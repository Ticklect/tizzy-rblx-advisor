# Live-ops brain

## Patch vs update

**Patch:** bug, exploit, hotfix, friction, urgent balance, small monetization fix. Ship when needed.

**Update:** intentional content/mechanic change designed to alter engagement/retention/play days/social activity/meaningful spend. Should have a player-behavior hypothesis.

Do not delay a critical anti-cheat fix for an arbitrary weekly update day.

## Four evidence streams

1. **Qualitative:** Discord, group/community forum, Creator Dashboard feedback, support/bug reports.
2. **Quantitative:** Home Recommendation metrics, funnels, retention, monetization, device/source segments.
3. **Competitive:** comparable games, update patterns, mechanics, community behavior.
4. **Core audience:** dedicated sweats/playtesters; ask what else they play and why they care about your game.

Use discernment: the core knows the game deeply but is not automatically a game designer.

## Core-audience research

Useful practices from the public live-ops case study:
- dedicated core playtester group;
- bounce update ideas off them;
- ask what other games they play;
- study those games;
- observe server tags/community overlap where appropriate;
- watch small YouTube uploads from ordinary players to spot real friction.

## Metric-targeted live ops

Examples:
- bounce -> onboarding/title-promise/FTUE
- playtime -> events, collection, meaningful activity (genre-dependent)
- play days -> reasons to return / social / progression
- D7 -> enough meaningful content/mechanics
- D28 -> durable progression/identity/community/mastery

Do not copy the mechanism blindly across genres.

## Practical cadence model

A public example used a weekly rhythm: launch/update day -> plan next -> assign/build -> review -> internal test -> core test -> launch. Treat this as one operational example, not a universal schedule. The principle is **plan early and leave real test time**.

## Do

- talk to players;
- play your own game;
- watch other people play;
- test updates before launch;
- have a clear metric/behavior target;
- keep improving even when revenue is good.

## Don't

- prioritize monetization over long-term gameplay health;
- ship last-minute untested changes;
- implement every community suggestion;
- try to please everyone;
- ship filler merely to claim weekly updates;
- assume a studio/outsourcing partner automatically understands your player base.
