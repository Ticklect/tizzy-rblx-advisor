# Market-research brain

## Goal

Find **proven demand + an execution/market gap**. Do not start from "what random idea can I invent?" and do not copy the biggest front-page game after the market is saturated.

## Research routes from the corpus

### 1. Rising smaller Roblox game
Look for a recently released experience with unusually strong CCU relative to visits, visible upward trajectory, manageable development scope, and evidence of player demand. Then play it deeply.

### 2. Underserved / poorly served loop
A game may have a strong core loop but weak dev execution: bugs, no updates, poor mobile UX, weak progression. That gap can be more useful than chasing the biggest current hit.

### 3. Proven fantasy + new loop
Take an audience fantasy with obvious demand and combine it with a genre/mechanic that market has not exhausted.

### 4. Old loop revival
Find an old/dead Roblox experience with a fundamentally fun loop and modernize controls, mobile support, UX, progression, packaging, content and live ops.

### 5. Adjacent-media mining
Study YouTube/TikTok/Steam/CrazyGames/mobile/Minecraft/Garry's Mod/etc. for high-demand fantasies or mechanics Roblox does not serve well.

### 6. Loop transplant
Take a proven loop from one demographic/fantasy and move it into another where it feels fresh.

### 7. AI-buildability filter
For small AI-assisted projects, look for low development time + high demand + low competition + evidence that a similarly scoped experience can work. Do not assume AI removes the need for UX/game design.

## Demand signals

Useful clues: CCU, visit velocity, age of experience, update cadence, audience comments, external views/wishlists/downloads, repeat engagement, comparable successes.

Do not rely on a single number. Cross-reference.

## Play the game deeply

Do not research from thumbnails alone. Study:
- core loop;
- progression;
- D1/D7 reasons to return;
- content exhaustion;
- monetization;
- bugs/feedback;
- mobile/console UX;
- social systems;
- why players keep playing after novelty ends.

## Meaningful twist

A theme swap is not enough. The twist should alter at least one important building block: fantasy, unique mechanic, tension, progression, social structure, map interaction, risk/reward, collection, or audience.

## Market answer shape

Demand proof -> saturation/competition -> gap -> proven loop -> twist -> development difficulty -> MVP -> kill criteria -> what evidence would make us change our mind.
